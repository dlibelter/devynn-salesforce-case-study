AI shouldn’t make the call. It should move the information.

What finance teams actually want from Salesforce’s AI, and the 12 weeks of research that uncovered it.

Role

UX Research intern, on a team of 4

Timeline

12 weeks, Spring 2026

Partner

Salesforce, through my UXR class at UC Berkeley MIMS

Methods

Competitive map, 5 interviews, affinity mapping, directional survey (12 responses)

01The ask

Salesforce Financial Services Cloud wanted AI to be the next big selling point for its finance clients, and the idea on the table was to automate financial decision-making and executive judgment, as long as it didn’t end up as just another dashboard.

12 weeks 4 person team Limited budget and privacy constraints
02Where Salesforce sits

Dominant for years, now under pressure.

Salesforce has dominated Financial Cloud systems historically, but the rise of AI and AI-centered competition is pushing them to evolve to remain dominant.

03Research

Start with the problems, not the AI.

AI was the suggested solution. I wanted to begin with where Salesforce FSC is falling short today, so I ran semi-structured interviews to uncover the biggest pain points clients face.

  • Q1What are the biggest points of friction in day-to-day work?
  • Q2Why do they happen?
  • Q3Where could it improve?

Five interviews, with everyone’s identity kept confidential.

Why manual work sticks around

“With the new tools come the need for redesigns of the workflows.”
“…when you introduce a new technology… you need to redesign the entire workflow. And this is the stressful part, because you need to redesign the roles and responsibilities.”
“…these people who already work 10 hours a day or 15 hours a day… are fed up with everything. They just won’t get the buy in and the time.”

Hunting for data

“I gotta go check back in, look at the source of the data… but also source from who actually entered it in that system.”
“That data and that information was obviously sourced by someone else. It comes to me.”
“My typical day to day is a lot on conference calls and spreadsheets.”

Trust needs a person

“You mess with people’s money. You mess with their emotions… You had to build rapport with people in order to have them trust you.”
“AI can have hallucinations or it doesn’t understand the questions. The human aspect isn’t in AI yet.”
“In the end of the day, it’s… less about the system and more about the person.”

Judgement can’t be reduced to numbers

“Obviously, there were reputational risks, political risks, as well as financial and legal risk that we needed to consider.”
“The gut feeling. I think some people make really gut feelings. That isn’t necessarily something that could be automated.”
“There is also always the risk of the person who is in charge, and the decision, how they affect the business processes.”

High stakes, hard to hand over

“Any inaccuracy in the quarterly earnings report… could be very bad for the stock price.”
“It would be hard for AI to call these risks out… mistakes fall back on our reputation.”
[When asked what part of his work he would trust to be fully automated] “None.”

Open to AI on tedious, lower-risk work

“Forecasting resources is manual labor… A model that can predict the information… would be amazing.”
“Note taking AI popped up here on Zoom… those are actually very cool.”
“That type of sort of tedious grunt work AI can do and can do better.”

Secure access, from anywhere

“I would like to see… a system in place that was secure enough to be legally covering all of the standards needed… to work from the office or from home.”
“Pain points within job: have to go back to the same person again and again and again…”

Hand-off break down

“A lot gets lost in email; sometimes the comprehension isn’t there.”
“The root of that is working with resources across the globe… only get about two hours of overlap.”
“It’s really just people issues… less about the system and more about the person.”

A small survey pointed the same way.

I created a small, directional survey with 12 responses. It supports our interview findings in a few ways:

Lose 5+ hours a week to manual work7/12
Say data entry would benefit most from automation6/12
Cite no shared platform across teams12/12
Cite misaligned incentives9/12

Recruiting was limited by our resources, so I used the survey to support the interview patterns, not to prove them.

04Findings

Three findings that changed the brief.

1

Manual sourcing gap

Time goes to hunting for data that other teams sourced but never uploaded to a central system.

2

Incentive misalignment

Departments track data differently based on their own incentives, creating friction at hand-offs.

3

Mismatched expectations

Salesforce assumed clients wanted judgment automation. Clients wanted the tedious work automated, with judgment reserved for humans who are accountable for it.

Salesforce assumed

The next level of AI was automating high-stakes decisions.

But their clients want

Automation they can truly trust on the repetitive, tedious work.

05My Recommendations

Re-imagine AI tools as a communication & information super-highway.

The opportunity

Instead of automating judgment, make AI the most trusted tool for moving information between teams, with the human keeping the final call.

A concept to test, not a validated design. Hover or tap a principle to see where it lives.

06Learnings

The research told us no.

Good user research is courageous. It’s loyal to the user’s needs, and disloyal to your expectations.

What we expected

AI that automates high-stakes decisions.

What users needed

AI that moves information, while people keep the call.

07Next Steps
1

Prototype it

An AI-powered Information Record & Retrieval Interface.

2

Test Usability & Acceptance

How much capture people in regulated finance will accept, and can be naturally integrated into their existing workflow?

3

Measure success

Hours saved per week and trust in retrieved data.
